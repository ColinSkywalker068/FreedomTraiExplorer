# Historical sources — Boston, 1770

Verified 2 November 2026. The scene is a curated late-December 1770 reconstruction, not a claim that these people gathered together. All witness dialogue is modern paraphrase unless explicitly identified otherwise. Contemporary testimony, editorial argument, archival descriptions and later scholarship are distinguished in the canonical registry.

## Access and method

Primary excerpts were inspected in the supplied TeachTN trial PDF (two pages), including Wyatt, Prince, Whitehouse and Palmes. The PDF cites Colonial Society of Massachusetts Publications (1905), vol. 7, pp. 8–9; Legal Papers of John Adams (1965), vol. 3, pp. 46–98; and Discovering the American Past (2002), pp. 81–88. These are edited excerpts rather than a complete trial transcript. Jane Whitehouse is connected to Jane Crothers through the MHS source-set marriage/context section. No political loyalty or psychological biography is inferred from her marriage.

The Virginia Tech Wyat deposition initially timed out, then was independently fetched and read by the project’s coordinating researcher (passage lines 42–49). That successful fetch supports C01–C02. The PDF spelling Wyatt is not silently treated as a different witness.

MHS database pages for the pamphlets failed, so their institutional descriptions in MHS History Source are explicitly identified as substitutes. These publication-history clues are not direct verification of all 96 or 31 depositions. The 31-testimony collection is not equated to the 26-item subset discussed in scholarship. The modern reenactment video is not an independent witness.

The supplied essay was not found by the project PDF search; only data/raw/official-freedom-trail-map.pdf appeared. The user’s essay summary informs the design, not collectible facts. Modern scholarship remains outside NPC knowledge. No archive login or paywall was bypassed.

## Deliberate limits

The March 19 fifth-coffin comparison and Edward Payne injury clue were not shipped: their proposed sources were not sufficiently inspected in this pass. C09 instead concerns Whitehouse’s description of clothing. The optional Palmes NPC is not implemented; his verified excerpt is available through an archival environment interaction. The two pamphlets and Gazette use clearly labeled modern reading cards, never generated historical-looking text presented as original. The Fair Account is an archival encounter because physical availability in Boston by the scene date is not verified.

The October 30 Preston acquittal and soldiers’ split verdict are supported by NPS. The Rotunda table of contents independently dates Verdicts to 5 December 1770; its documents require registration. The journal avoids claiming that legal outcomes prove the identity of every speaker. NPS’s general exposition is not attributed as words spoken by witnesses.
